Step 1 — See what's actually on your reports

Your score is calculated from your reports at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. Pull all three free at AnnualCreditReport.com, the federally authorized site. Mark anything you don't recognize: accounts that aren't yours, late payments that weren't late, wrong balances, debts listed twice.

FICO publishes the five factors and their weights:

Payment history — 35%Paying on time is the single biggest lever.
Amounts owed — 30%How much of your available credit you're using.
Length of history — 15%Older accounts help; think twice before closing them.
Credit mix — 10%A blend of cards, loans, and other accounts.
New credit — 10%Many applications in a short window can drag you down.

Step 2 — Dispute what's wrong, in writing

Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, bureaus must investigate disputed items and remove anything they can't verify. Three letters do most of the work — the same three in the CreditFix Kit:

  • Bureau error disputes — for accounts, balances, or late marks that are simply incorrect.
  • Collector validation requests — a collector must prove the debt is yours before it belongs on your report.
  • Goodwill adjustments — a specific ask to a creditor you otherwise pay on time to remove a one-off late mark.

Send disputes in writing, keep copies, and repeat for each bureau — an error fixed at one often still sits on the other two.

Step 3 — Rebuild month by month

  • Autopay at least the minimum on every account, so a new late mark never lands.
  • Pay cards down early in the cycle so the balance reported to the bureaus is lower.
  • Ask for credit-limit increases on cards you already have instead of opening new ones.
  • Add one small, on-time payment stream — a credit-builder account — and let it age.

Be wary of cards aimed at low scores that bury you in sign-up and monthly fees before you've spent a dollar — read the fee schedule first, every time.
